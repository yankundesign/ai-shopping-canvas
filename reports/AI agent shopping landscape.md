# Agentic shopping leaves the shortlist unclaimed

As of 24 November 2026, AI-assisted shopping is a mainstream way to research and compare and a minor way to buy, and no one has shipped the agent-agnostic "shopping canvas" this project describes. Every major assistant now renders product cards, and three of them (Meta Muse, ChatGPT Dots, Gemini Task mode) run agents that pay with a tokenized card after explicit approval, yet eMarketer counts only about 1.5% of US e-commerce completing on AI platforms and surveys put the share of consumers willing to let an agent pay unsupervised at 9% to 16%. The neglected part is the middle of the funnel: candidates live in a scrolling chat thread, comparison is a table the model generates on request, and the only persistent holders in general assistants are Google's Universal Cart and ChatGPT's Favorites, both launched in October 2026 and both borrowed from cart and wishlist conventions. The closest products (Daydream's "clothing rack", Onton's furniture canvas, Mindtrip's collections, Google's travel Canvas) are closed, vertical and filled from one company's catalog or agent. The technology to build an open version exists today: MCP Apps renders third-party UI inside Claude (including phones), ChatGPT, Copilot and others, UCP and ACP define near-identical cart objects with a hand-off URL, and Shopify's catalog is searchable without an API key. The opening is real but narrow. Standalone "save and collect" tools have failed as businesses, affiliate revenue is thin and fragile, and hosts can absorb the feature. The realistic first form is an open-source MCP server with a persistent board and click-out checkout, which is a strong portfolio project; a product needs a hosted tier and a wedge beyond the board itself. Cyber Week 2026 started the day this report was written, so no measured holiday results exist yet.

## Assistants show cards in a scrolling thread, and only two keep anything

The assistants have separated "chat that recommends" from "agent that buys". ChatGPT's ordinary chat and Claude are discovery surfaces that leave checkout to the merchant. Google, Microsoft and Perplexity run wallet checkout inside the assistant. A new class of personal agents drives a browser in a cloud VM and pays with a single-use credential once the user approves. OpenAI's path shows the shift most clearly. It launched single-item Instant Checkout in September 2025, said in March 2026 that it "did not offer the level of flexibility that we aspire to provide", and pivoted to visual product browsing with merchant-run checkout ([OpenAI](https://openai.com/index/powering-product-discovery-in-chatgpt/)). It then retired the Atlas browser in August, launched always-on "Dots" agents on 29 September, added product Favorites on 1 October, and on 13 November began a limited rollout of **ChatGPT Wallet for US Pro subscribers inside Dots conversations only**, where the user reviews merchant, items, total and card and authorizes with Face ID or Touch ID ([ChatGPT release notes](https://help.openai.com/en/articles/6825453-chatgpt-release-notes); [TechCrunch](https://techcrunch.com/2026/11/13/chatgpts-new-wallet-lets-ai-agents-shop-and-check-out-for-you/)). Meta launched Muse on 8 September 2026 as a personal agent in its own app and inside WhatsApp that books travel and checks out with a one-time Stripe Link card, asking before purchases ([Meta](https://about.fb.com/news/2026/09/introducing-muse-personal-ai-agent/)); TechCrunch reports **more than 10 million users**, with shopping a top activity ([TechCrunch](https://techcrunch.com/2026/11/13/chatgpts-new-wallet-lets-ai-agents-shop-and-check-out-for-you/)).

Google has the most complete consumer stack. It added hotel booking inside AI Mode on 27 August 2026 (choose a room, review the cancellation policy, pay with Google Pay, with Booking.com, Expedia, Hilton, Marriott and others as partners) ([Google](https://blog.google/products-and-platforms/products/search/book-travel-ai-mode/)), launched a cross-retailer Universal Cart on 15 October ([Google](https://blog.google/products-and-platforms/products/shopping/universal-cart-launch/)), and on 17 November upgraded a free Gemini "Task" tab that runs multistep jobs such as comparing product listings in an isolated VM and asks before a purchase ([SiliconANGLE](https://siliconangle.com/2026/11/18/google-upgrades-geminis-agentic-task-mode-with-new-model-e-commerce-features/)). Anthropic has stayed out of payments. Claude gained everyday connectors in April 2026 (Booking.com, StubHub, Resy, Instacart, Viator, Uber and others), is "designed to check with you first" before booking or buying, and promises to stay ad-free ([Anthropic](https://claude.com/resources/articles/connectors-for-everyday-life)). An industry observer found Claude serving "rudimentary" product cards across categories from mid-June 2026, which Anthropic has not announced or documented ([Retailgentic](https://www.retailgentic.com/p/breaking-claude-has-product-cards)). Claude in Chrome became a Cowork side panel in August and still asks before a purchase even in auto-approve mode ([Anthropic](https://claude.com/blog/cowork-chrome-side-panel)). Perplexity's Instant Buy with PayPal dates from November 2025 ([eWeek](https://www.eweek.com/news/paypal-perplexity-instant-buy-shopping/)), and a third-party tracker found no 2026 update to it ([Sanbi.ai](https://sanbi.ai/blog/agentic-commerce-tracker-ai-checkout)). Microsoft launched Copilot Checkout in January 2026 ([Axios](https://www.axios.com/2026/01/08/microsoft-ai-copilot-checkout)). Manus, independent again after China blocked Meta's acquisition ([TechStartups](https://techstartups.com/2026/09/01/ai-startup-manus-resumes-independent-operations-after-china-kills-metas-2-billion-deal/)), offers a Browser Operator that acts in the user's logged-in browser but documents no shopping display, wallet or checkout flow ([Manus](https://manus.im/features/manus-browser-operator)). Almost every transactional feature is US-only.

Three patterns matter for this project. First, results scroll away. Outside Universal Cart and Favorites, a candidate exists only in the message that rendered it, and each refinement produces a new set of cards further down the thread. Trade press calls the result a shortlist with "no page two": a product is either in the assistant's pick or invisible ([ChannelX](https://channelx.world/2026/11/this-is-the-first-holiday-season-where-the-shopping-happens-in-chat/)). Second, comparison is an output and not an interaction. ChatGPT and Google generate side-by-side tables, but no assistant documents a way for the user to pick three items, pin a row, drop a candidate or carry a comparison forward, and Google's Universal Cart launch post mentions no comparison tools at all ([Google](https://blog.google/products-and-platforms/products/shopping/universal-cart-launch/)). Third, the richest decision surfaces are desktop-bound while approval is mobile-first. Google's Canvas, the only shipped canvas-style panel, covers travel planning only and runs on desktop in US Labs ([Google](https://blog.google/products/search/agentic-plans-booking-travel-canvas-ai-mode/)), and Claude in Chrome has no mobile version ([Anthropic](https://claude.com/blog/cowork-chrome-side-panel)), whereas Wallet approval, Google Pay and Muse-in-WhatsApp all assume a phone.

Tickets and hotels follow the same shape with less polish. ChatGPT shows restaurant times "directly in the response" ([ChatGPT release notes](https://help.openai.com/en/articles/6825453-chatgpt-release-notes)), and Google returns event tickets as "a curated list with direct links" to Ticketmaster, StubHub, SeatGeek and Vivid Seats ([Google](https://blog.google/products/search/agentic-plans-booking-travel-canvas-ai-mode/)). The research found no confirmed flow that completes an event-ticket purchase inside any assistant. A July 2026 hands-on test found the Booking.com, Expedia and Viator apps in ChatGPT return live listings with deep-linked booking buttons, but ChatGPT repeatedly skipped connected apps until pushed, while Claude proactively suggested relevant connectors ([Skift](https://skift.com/2026/07/06/chatgpt-apps-claude-connectors-travel-hands-on-test/)).

The documented complaints are commercial more than visual. Walmart said shoppers feared receiving "five boxes" from single-item checkout and that in-chat purchases converted three times worse than click-outs ([Modern Retail](https://www.modernretail.co/technology/what-went-wrong-with-chatgpts-instant-checkout/)). Muse users report blocks or failed purchases at Amazon, Walmart, eBay, Yelp and airlines ([TechCrunch](https://techcrunch.com/2026/10/06/the-next-hurdle-for-ai-agents-getting-websites-to-let-them-in/)). OpenAI itself warns that shopping research "may make mistakes about details such as price and availability" ([OpenAI](https://openai.com/index/chatgpt-shopping-research/)). Public critique has focused on whether agents can buy and whether retailers will let them. The research found no NN/g or Baymard study of browsing and comparing inside a general chat assistant, so the claim that users lose candidates in long threads is a well-supported inference and not a measured finding.

## Retailers built their own agents, kept the checkout and split three ways on outsiders

The measurable volume sits in first-party assistants. Amazon renamed Rufus to Alexa for Shopping on 13 May 2026, put it in the search bar, and added price tracking, conditional purchases and Buy for Me, which completes orders on other retailers' sites with the shopper's stored address and card ([TechCrunch](https://techcrunch.com/2026/05/13/amazon-launches-an-ai-shopping-assistant-for-the-search-bar-powered-by-alexa/)). On its Q3 call Amazon said **more than 400 million customers used it in the prior 12 months** and that users spend 50% more ([Modern Retail](https://www.modernretail.co/technology/amazon-says-more-than-400m-customers-have-used-alexa-for-shopping-in-the-last-12-months/)). Walmart reported Sparky usage up 129% year over year with 40% larger baskets ([PYMNTS](https://pymnts.com/earnings/2026/walmart-pairs-membership-growth-with-an-ai-agent-push)). These lift figures are correlational; assistant users are plausibly higher-intent shoppers to begin with. Sales arriving from outside assistants remain tiny wherever they are disclosed: Etsy put agentic traffic under 1% of total ([PYMNTS](https://www.pymnts.com/earnings/2026/etsy-sells-depop-pushes-ai-and-mobile-to-restart-growth/)), Booking.com said referrals from ChatGPT-style platforms were well under 1% of room nights ([PhocusWire](https://www.phocuswire.com/news/finance/booking-holdings-q3-2026-earnings)), and Expedia's CEO called agentic traffic "very small so far" ([PYMNTS](https://www.pymnts.com/earnings/2026/expedia-says-travelers-plan-with-ai-agents-but-book-direct/)).

Retail platforms present options much as the assistants do: a chat panel over the existing search and product pages, with comparison on request. Amazon comes closest to a deliberate shortlist step. Shoppers select several products from search results and the agent compares them side by side, and it can build a buying guide across Amazon and other sites ([Digital Commerce 360](https://www.digitalcommerce360.com/2026/05/13/amazon-launches-ai-agent-alexa-for-shopping/)); since 29 October the agent can also create and edit lists and registries ([About Amazon](https://www.aboutamazon.com/news/retail/alexa-for-shopping-new-capabilities)). No platform in the research has shipped a dedicated AI shortlist or comparison board; persistence is delegated to lists, wishlists and carts. Two travel examples are the most instructive precedents for a canvas. Airbnb's September 2026 AI search generates summaries that compare properties the user has already saved to a wishlist, and Brian Chesky has deliberately avoided a chatbot interface ([TechCrunch](https://techcrunch.com/2026/09/30/airbnb-adds-ai-search-more-social-features/)). Expedia dropped its all-in-one Romie chatbot for narrow agents and now runs a "compare agent" and a "property expert agent" inside the booking flow ([Skift](https://skift.com/?p=838262); [PYMNTS](https://www.pymnts.com/earnings/2026/expedia-says-travelers-plan-with-ai-agents-but-book-direct/)). Both put AI to work over a set the human curated, which is the canvas idea inside one brand's walls.

Toward outside agents the market has three postures. Amazon is closed. Its robots.txt carried 101 user-agent blocks as of 22 September 2026, and on 20 September it began showing Muse users a block page saying "Continued access by an unauthorized AI agent violates Amazon's Conditions of Use" ([Search Engine Journal](https://www.searchenginejournal.com/amazon-blocked-metas-muse-and-robots-txt-had-nothing-to-say/590494/)). Andy Jassy said Amazon expects Alexa for Shopping to be "the primary shopping agent customers use to shop Amazon" while anticipating work with horizontal agents ([Modern Retail](https://www.modernretail.co/technology/amazon-says-more-than-400m-customers-have-used-alexa-for-shopping-in-the-last-12-months/)). eBay bans "buy-for-me agents, LLM-driven bots, or any end-to-end flow that attempts to place orders without human review" without its permission ([PPC Land](https://ppc.land/ebay-bans-the-robot-shopper-unless-ebay-sends-the-invitation/)). Walmart is open through a gate: approved agents connect through an Agent Gateway with per-agent profiles and revocable access ([Customer Experience Dive](https://www.customerexperiencedive.com/news/walmart-is-building-an-omnichannel-environment-around-agentic-ai/875251/)), and customers can cap an outside agent's spending at $50, $100 or $200 ([Modern Retail](https://www.modernretail.co/?p=175624)). Shopify is open infrastructure. It syndicates merchant catalogs to ChatGPT, Copilot, Google and the Shop app by default ([Shopify](https://www.shopify.com/news/spring-26-edition-merchant)), exposes hosted MCP servers for catalog search, carts and checkout ([Shopify dev docs](https://shopify.dev/docs/agents)), and on 28 September 2026 opened checkout to browser-based agents through WebMCP ([TechCrunch](https://techcrunch.com/2026/09/28/shopify-opens-checkout-to-browser-based-ai-agents/)).

The legal baseline moved in August 2026. The Ninth Circuit vacated Amazon's injunction against Perplexity's Comet, reasoning that when an agent acts at a user's direction the user, not the vendor, is accessing Amazon ([Engadget](https://engadget.com/2230471/perplexity-has-successfully-overturned-amazon-injunction-on-its-ai-shopping-bot)). The case continues, and Amazon and eBay now lean on customer-binding terms and server-side detection, which shifts risk onto the shopper's account. Stated reasons for blocking are security and consent; analysts attribute the stance to advertising, with Amazon's Q3 2026 ad services at $20 billion ([Modern Retail](https://www.modernretail.co/?p=175624)). No executive in the sources names ad revenue as the reason. The revealed preference is consistent across retail and travel: keep the account, keep the checkout, keep the loyalty link. That preference favors a canvas whose purchase step is a click to the merchant, and it means card data for Amazon's catalog depends on whatever the user's own agent or browser can legitimately supply.

## The nearest canvases are closed, vertical and filled from one catalog

Startups have built boards, racks and collections, but each is a destination tied to its own catalog or agent. Daydream is the closest behavioral match and the newest. On 17 November 2026 it launched a fashion agent whose shoppers save pieces to a persistent "clothing rack" to compare before deciding, reuse one photo for virtual try-on across the rack, watch an agent task list, and approve each one-click purchase at partner retailers ([PR Newswire](https://prnewswire.com/news-releases/daydream-launches-first-ever-autonomous-agent-for-fashion-shopping-styling-and-checkout-302941544.html)). It is one week old, so no reception data exists. Onton is the only product explicitly built as an infinite canvas for shopping: users drag furniture from a 3-million-item catalog onto a workspace alongside inspiration images and AI room renders. Its founder wrote that search and image generation are the hard problems, "not the canvas itself", and the Show HN post drew 8 points ([Hacker News](https://news.ycombinator.com/item?id=45933456)). Mindtrip combines chat, cards, a map and shareable collections for travel ([Mindtrip](https://mindtrip.ai/)). Pinterest launched the experimental Ask Pinterest app in June 2026, which personalizes from saved Pins and Boards, and its MCP server serves advertisers and not shoppers ([TechCrunch](https://techcrunch.com/2026/06/17/pinterest-launches-an-experimental-ai-shopping-app-called-ask-pinterest/)).

|---|---|---|---|---|
| Daydream rack | Fashion pieces for comparison and try-on | Daydream's agent, from its own catalog | No | Only inside Daydream |
| Onton Canvas | Furniture, inspiration images, AI renders | The human, dragging from Onton's catalog | No | No |
| Mindtrip | Places in collections with a map | Mindtrip's assistant and the user | No | Only inside Mindtrip |

| mcp-apps-shopping-demo | Product-grid widget with shared cart state | Any MCP host | Yes | Yes, but archived at 5 stars |

Venture money has moved away from consumer shopping surfaces. One tracker reports disclosed AI-shopping funding fell from $291.2 million to $181.8 million year over year with a median round of $2.91 million ([newmarketpitch](https://newmarketpitch.com/blogs/news/ai-shopping-funding-analysis)), while general-purpose agent Instinct raised a **$250 million Series B at a $2.5 billion valuation** roughly four months after founding ([Value Add VC](https://valueaddvc.com/pulse/ai-shopping-agents-startups-instinct-daydream-phia-2026)). Infrastructure startups sell checkout execution (Rye), merchant enablement (Firmly, which raised a $15 million Series A in November 2026), agent payments (Basis Theory, Skyfire, Nekuda) and product data (Channel3), and none owns a consumer display layer ([PYMNTS](https://www.pymnts.com/news/artificial-intelligence/2026/firmly-lands-15-million-dollars-investors-back-merchant-side-agentic-commerce/); [Rye](https://rye.com/blog/agentic-commerce-startups)). Rye's landscape map, written by a participant, lists no UI or decision-surface category at all.

Open source is rich at the protocol and generic-UI layers and nearly empty at the specific artifact. Anthropic's `commerce-agents` blueprint has about 3,500 stars but targets a business embedding an agent in its own storefront ([GitHub](https://github.com/anthropics/commerce-agents)). The one cross-host shopping widget, `openmobilehub/mcp-apps-shopping-demo`, proves a single UI bundle can render a product grid with shared cart state in Claude, ChatGPT and Goose, and it is archived with 5 stars ([GitHub](https://github.com/openmobilehub/mcp-apps-shopping-demo)). A week-old project, `browsa-io/open-shopping-agent`, compares across retailers and fills carts in the user's own Chrome with an approval gate ([GitHub](https://github.com/browsa-io/open-shopping-agent)). A GitHub search for "shopping canvas" returned nothing relevant ([GitHub search](https://github.com/search?q=shopping+canvas&type=repositories)). No project combines a tool surface an agent can push cards to, a persistent board the human rearranges and shortlists, and a read-back tool for the human's choices. The same pattern holds in star counts: generic generative-UI frameworks draw 15,000 to 39,000 stars, commerce protocols 1,600 to 3,500, and shopper-side tools under 250, so developer demand for this exact artifact is unproven.

The failures are the most useful evidence. Carted raised $13 million, pivoted from a universal commerce API to an "ultimate wishlist" app, and shut down in October 2025 ([Startup Daily](https://www.startupdaily.net/topic/business/4-years-after-a-record-13m-seed-raise-blackbird-backed-ultimate-wishlist-shopping-app-carted-shuts-down/)). Microsoft removed Edge Collections in June 2026 in favor of Copilot ([Windows Latest](https://www.windowslatest.com/2026/06/06/microsoft-just-killed-edges-collections-and-sidebar-for-more-copilot-after-years-of-pushing-both-features/)). Phia, the best-funded consumer shopping extension, was accused in July 2026 of taking affiliate credit on purchases it did not drive ([TechCrunch](https://techcrunch.com/2026/07/10/phia-accused-of-cookie-stuffing-taking-affiliate-credit-on-purchases-it-didnt-earn/)). Expedia acquired trip planner Layla in July 2026 ([Skift](https://skift.com/2026/07/31/expedia-acquired-ai-trip-planner-layla-exclusive/)). A collection feature survives when it is attached to a larger loop such as a feed, a checkout or an agent. A board by itself has not sustained a company.

## MCP Apps plus a hosted link is the buildable stack, with payment left to merchants

An independent developer can build the display and selection layers on open standards today. MCP Apps is the common rendering mechanism. A tool declares a `ui://` HTML resource, the host renders it in a sandboxed iframe, and the view talks to the host over JSON-RPC; hosts without support fall back to the plain tool result ([MCP Apps spec](https://github.com/modelcontextprotocol/ext-apps/blob/main/specification/2026-01-26/apps.mdx)). The community client matrix lists Claude web and desktop, ChatGPT, VS Code Copilot, Microsoft 365 Copilot, Cursor and Goose as supporting it, and Claude Code as not ([MCP client matrix](https://modelcontextprotocol.io/extensions/client-matrix)). Claude renders apps on iOS and Android in a native WebView and lets any user add a custom connector by pasting a server URL, with no Anthropic review ([Claude design guidelines](https://claude.com/docs/connectors/building/mcp-apps/design-guidelines); [Claude docs](https://claude.com/docs/connectors/building/directory-vs-custom)). ChatGPT implements the same standard with extras layered on `window.openai` ([OpenAI](https://developers.openai.com/apps-sdk/mcp-apps-in-chatgpt)). Consumer Gemini is the gap: Google documents MCP Apps for Gemini Enterprise only and steers developers toward A2UI, a declarative protocol that reached 1.0 on 11 November 2026 ([Google Cloud](https://cloud.google.com/blog/topics/developers-practitioners/devs-guide-to-mcp-apps-in-gemini/); [Google Developers](https://developers.googleblog.com/en/a2ui-10-build-delightful-fast-reliable-agentic-experiences/)). ChatGPT's mobile behavior and its availability to unpublished consumer apps were not confirmed from a primary source and need testing.

The word "canvas" collides with inline limits. Both Claude and ChatGPT cap an inline carousel at 3 to 8 items with an image, a title, a few lines of metadata and one call to action per card ([Claude design guidelines](https://claude.com/docs/connectors/building/mcp-apps/design-guidelines); [OpenAI UI guidelines](https://developers.openai.com/apps-sdk/concepts/ui-guidelines)). Inline height is capped, and on mobile a vertical pan inside an inline app scrolls the conversation, so a real compare-and-arrange surface must request fullscreen. On 10 November 2026 Anthropic let apps open beside the chat or full screen on web and desktop and let Claude act inside them through app tools, with Shopify listed as "in development"; the post does not mention mobile for these features ([Anthropic](https://claude.com/blog/interactive-connectors-full-tool)).

Handing a selection back to the agent has three standard channels. The view can call server tools directly, including tools marked app-only that the model cannot see. It can call `ui/update-model-context`, which silently replaces what the model knows about the view and which OpenAI calls "the portable way to send selections or staged edits to the model". It can send `ui/message`, a user-role message that triggers a turn ([MCP Apps spec](https://github.com/modelcontextprotocol/ext-apps/blob/main/specification/2026-01-26/apps.mdx); [OpenAI](https://developers.openai.com/apps-sdk/mcp-apps-in-chatgpt)). Each has a limit. Model context is a single overwrite slot. Claude delivers an app message only with the user's agreement, text only, up to 8,000 characters ([Claude docs](https://claude.com/docs/connectors/building/mcp-apps/send-messages)). Nothing in the widget persists: "store on your server anything that you need to keep" ([Claude design guidelines](https://claude.com/docs/connectors/building/mcp-apps/design-guidelines)). The robust design follows from these limits. Every click writes to the server through an app-only tool, the view mirrors a compact snapshot of the whole shortlist into model context, and one explicit button ("Have the agent buy these") sends a message. Server-side state keyed by a board ID also solves the fallback case. An agent that cannot render UI (Claude Code, CLI and API agents, messaging bots) returns a link to the same hosted board and reads the human's choices back with an ordinary tool, the polling pattern Google recommends for long-running app state ([Google Cloud](https://cloud.google.com/blog/topics/developers-practitioners/devs-guide-to-mcp-apps-in-gemini/)). No standard yet notifies an agent that a human has finished on a hosted page.

For data, two commerce specs supply the shapes. UCP (Google and Shopify, spec version 2026-08-25, pushed daily) defines catalog product and variant objects and a cart with `line_items`, `totals` and a `continue_url` meant for cart handoff, sharing and human-in-the-loop flows ([UCP Cart spec](https://ucp.dev/latest/specification/shopping/cart/); [UCP Catalog spec](https://ucp.dev/latest/specification/shopping/catalog/)). ACP (OpenAI and Stripe) defines a near-identical cart, though its repo was last pushed in July 2026 ([ACP repo](https://github.com/agentic-commerce-protocol/agentic-commerce-protocol)). Neither defines a "shortlist", a compare set, or human annotations such as notes and rejections; that schema is open design space. Lodging exists in UCP only as a draft Booking capability ([ucp.dev](https://ucp.dev/)), and the research found no standard for event tickets, so hotel and ticket cards need a project-defined schema. Shopify's Global Catalog MCP offers `search_catalog`, `lookup_catalog` and `get_product` with no API key, at rate limits that keyless access cannot raise ([Shopify](https://shopify.dev/docs/agents/catalog)).

Payment is where independence ends. "Completing checkout within your agent requires Shopify's permission" ([Shopify](https://shopify.dev/docs/agents)). Stripe's Agentic Commerce Suite is a private preview requiring an agreement with each seller, and Stripe warns that agents offering both discovery and purchase "might be considered marketplace facilitators" with tax duties ([Stripe](https://docs.stripe.com/agentic-commerce/agents/embedded.md?agent-checkout-mode=full)). ChatGPT apps cannot collect new payment credentials ([OpenAI](https://developers.openai.com/apps-sdk/mcp-apps-in-chatgpt)). The path that needs no partner is hand-off: Shopify's cart and checkout permalinks, one store per link, with the merchant as merchant of record ([Shopify](https://shopify.dev/docs/agents/checkout)). A cross-merchant shortlist therefore has to group items by merchant and track one checkout per store. The second half of the project's loop, sending the selection back so the agent executes the purchase, requires no payment integration from the canvas: the user's own agent already holds the wallet (ChatGPT Wallet, Stripe Link in Muse, Google Wallet in Task mode), and the canvas only has to deliver a precise, structured selection.

Two engineering constraints deserve early attention. MCP Apps block all external origins by default and require declared domains ([MCP Apps spec](https://github.com/modelcontextprotocol/ext-apps/blob/main/specification/2026-01-26/apps.mdx)), and a board aggregating many merchants cannot enumerate every image host, so it needs an image proxy on one declared origin. Product content is also an attack surface. July 2026 research showed a planted product review causing an agent to click "Buy Now" instead of "Read More" ([The Hacker News](https://thehackernews.com/2026/07/new-agent-data-injection-attack-can.html)). The canvas should escape listing text in the UI and send the model IDs and human choices, never raw listing text as instructions. A human-facing board is itself a mitigation: it is the "trusted surface" role that AP2 names, where a person sees exact items, prices and merchant before anything is bought ([AP2 specification](https://ap2-protocol.org/ap2/specification/)). For the board UI, tldraw is the most capable canvas SDK but requires a production license, while a card grid on plain React or json-render avoids that constraint ([tldraw license](https://tldraw.dev/legal/tldraw-license); [json-render](https://github.com/vercel-labs/json-render)).

## Shoppers want AI to compare and themselves to approve, and the money is thin

Consumer evidence supports the canvas premise on behavior and undercuts it on revenue. Research with AI is now common: Bain puts US consumers using generative AI for product research and comparison at 30% to 45% ([Bain](https://www.bain.com/insights/2030-forecast-how-agentic-ai-will-reshape-us-retail-snap-chart/)), and Phocuswright found 56% of travelers used AI on at least one trip in the past year ([PhocusWire](https://www.phocuswire.com/news/online/shift-travel-behavior-ai-surge-phocuswright-research)). Visits that arrive from AI tools are valuable. Adobe reports that in September 2026 AI traffic to US retail sites converted **90% better than other traffic**, a reversal from 9% worse in August 2025 ([Retail TouchPoints](https://www.retailtouchpoints.com/?p=621887)). The likely mechanism is selection: people click through after they have already narrowed their choice. The one peer-reviewed study, on data ending July 2025, found the opposite ordering, with organic search converting about 13% better than ChatGPT referrals, which were under 0.2% of e-commerce traffic ([Marketing Science](https://pubsonline.informs.org/doi/full/10.1287/mksc.2025.0489)). Both results describe a channel where the purchase completes on the retailer's site. eMarketer counts about $19.7 billion of US e-commerce flowing through AI platforms in 2026, roughly 1.5%, and cut its outlook citing "tepid" interest in agents buying ([eMarketer](https://www.emarketer.com/content/retailers-hold-ai-commerce-advantage-over-chatgpt)); it forecasts only $1.44 billion completed end to end by agents ([Modern Retail](https://www.modernretail.co/?p=175624)).

Comparison is the task people most want from AI, and chat does not finish the job. Baymard's June 2026 survey of 2,273 US shoppers found 63% prefer AI chatbots for comparing product features ([Baymard](https://baymard.com/research-articles/ai-engagement-quantitative-ux-insights-2026)), and impact.com found product comparison the most common recent AI shopping activity at 40% ([impact.com](https://impact.com/resources/reports/ai-driven-buyer-journey/personalized-ai-shopping-agents-and-auto-buying/)). An IAB study of more than 450 screen-recorded sessions, from late 2025, found that AI lengthened the journey from 1.6 steps to 3.8, that 95% of shoppers took extra steps to feel confident, and that 78% visited a retailer or marketplace, where they checked prices, variants, reviews and availability ([Search Engine Journal](https://www.searchenginejournal.com/trust-in-ai-shopping-is-limited-as-shoppers-verify-on-websites/559497/)). A small UserTesting study (10 participants, 2025) found comparison tables with pictures were the most popular output, that participants valued choosing the criteria themselves, and that the journey "often broke down" at the handoff to a retailer ([UserTesting](https://www.usertesting.com/blog/ai-assisted-shopping-customer-journey)). Both behavioral studies predate ChatGPT's March 2026 visual update and may overstate current friction.

Older e-commerce research explains what a shortlist surface replaces. Baymard observed desktop users opening each product in its own tab and phone users rarely using multiple tabs, and recommends comparison views showing every spec for up to five products ([Baymard](https://baymard.com/research-articles/provide-comparison-features)). NN/g found in 2018 that shoppers used the cart as a "holding area" to compare and save items, and avoided wishlists that required registration or naming ([NN/g](https://www.nngroup.com/articles/wishlist-or-cart/)). Adobe forecasts mobile at 57.4% of US online holiday spend in 2026 ([Adobe](https://news.adobe.com/news/2026/09/adobe-us-holiday-shopping-season-to-hit-record)). The phone is where most spending happens and where the tab workaround is least available, which makes it the likeliest place for unmet need. That conclusion is an inference: no study in the research tested chat shopping on phones.

On delegation, the approval step is the product. Forrester found about three-quarters of online adults uncomfortable letting an agent complete and pay for a purchase, even with spending limits ([Forrester](https://www.forrester.com/blogs/consumers-arent-ready-to-delegate-payments-to-ai-agents/)). Accenture's 25,590-person survey found **32% would accept an agent that picks while they approve before payment, against 9% for autonomous purchasing**, with control mattering most for hotels, clothing and experiences ([AI News](https://www.artificialintelligence-news.com/news/ai-shopping-agents-consumer-trust-accenture-report/)). Deloitte found only 16% of US consumers trust agents to complete purchases for them ([Adweek](https://www.adweek.com/commerce/consumers-arent-ready-to-let-ai-do-their-holiday-shopping-yet/)), Okta found 67% unlikely to let an agent complete a purchase without review ([Modern Retail](https://www.modernretail.co/?p=175624)), and Riskified reports comfort with agent transactions fell during early 2026 ([Business Wire](https://www.businesswire.com/news/home/20260427900819/en/Riskified-Study-Finds-Consumers-Arent-Ready-to-Hand-Over-Control-as-AI-Transforms-Shopping-with-Over-Half-Afraid-of-Online-Fraud)). Willingness concentrates in grocery and household replenishment ([impact.com](https://impact.com/resources/reports/ai-driven-buyer-journey/personalized-ai-shopping-agents-and-auto-buying/)). A surface where the agent prepares and the human chooses matches where most consumers are, and it matters most in the categories this project names.

The business models available to an independent surface are weak. Amazon Associates pays 1% to 4.5% on most mainstream categories, with 4% on apparel and "all other" and 10% only on luxury beauty ([Amazon Associates](https://affiliate-program.amazon.com/help/node/topic/GRXPHT8U84RAYDXZ)), and an aggregator such as Skimlinks keeps 25% of the commission ([Skimlinks](https://support.skimlinks.com/hc/en-us/articles/360024626093-Why-doesn-t-the-commission-received-match-the-merchant-rate)). A $100 basket therefore yields about $2 to $4. Attribution breaks when an agent browses and no tagged click occurs, and only 13% of marketing leaders report a clear path from AI visibility to partner compensation ([eMarketer](https://emarketer.com/content/marketers-see-ai-s-influence-on-purchases--they-just-can-t-pay-it)). Phia shows what happens when a tool forces attribution: investigators documented hidden tabs overwriting other publishers' referral codes, and impact.com suspended it ([The Impression](https://theimpression.com/phias-affiliate-fraud-scandal-raises-questions-for-ai-shopping-agents/)). Transaction fees are not a dependable model either. OpenAI's reported 4% fee on Shopify sales lasted about two months before the Instant Checkout retreat ([PYMNTS](https://www.pymnts.com/news/ecommerce/2026/shopify-merchants-to-pay-4percent-fee-on-sales-made-through-chatgpt-checkout)). The large platforms monetize with advertising at a scale no independent tool can copy: OpenAI says ChatGPT Ads passed a $1 billion annualized run rate within 200 days ([OpenAI](https://openai.com/index/expanding-access-to-ai-with-chatgpt-ads/)). For open-source tools the pattern that pays is a hosted version, with sponsorship as a supplement, though the evidence is blog-level and includes no shopping-specific case ([PostHog](https://posthog.com/blog/open-source-business-models)).

Legal and platform risk attaches to the act of transacting, which a display layer avoids. Every documented dispute involves an agent that logs in and buys. eBay's and Shopify's policies single out completing payment without a final human review ([The Register](https://www.theregister.com/2026/01/22/ebay_updates_legalese_to_ban/); [Retail TouchPoints](https://www.retailtouchpoints.com/topics/digital-commerce/shopify-adds-guardrails-to-control-agentic-purchases)), and Amazon's amended complaint against Perplexity targets user cookies leaving the device for a vendor's cloud ([eWeek](https://www.eweek.com/news/perplexity-ai-shopping-agent-returns-amazon/)). A canvas that never holds retailer credentials, never drives checkout server-side and sends the human to the merchant's own page sits outside those prohibitions. It still inherits how each agent obtained its data: showing scraped Amazon prices can breach affiliate rules that reportedly require API-sourced prices cached for at most an hour ([ChatAds](https://www.getchatads.com/blog/amazon-associates-operating-agreement-ai-apps/)). Market forecasts do not change this picture. Estimates for US agentic commerce in 2030 run from eMarketer's single-digit share of e-commerce through Bain's $300 billion to $500 billion to McKinsey's "up to $1 trillion" ([Bain](https://www.bain.com/insights/2030-forecast-how-agentic-ai-will-reshape-us-retail-snap-chart/); [McKinsey](https://www.mckinsey.com/capabilities/quantumblack/our-insights/the-agentic-commerce-opportunity-how-ai-agents-are-ushering-in-a-new-era-for-consumers-and-merchants)). The spread comes from counting "influenced" versus completed purchases, and the only forecaster that revised in 2026 revised down. No forecast isolates the segment a canvas serves, which is AI-assisted research with a human-completed purchase.

## The opening is a neutral decision layer, and its first realistic form is open source

The white space is the presentation-and-decision layer between open data and checkout plumbing on one side and whatever agent the user already runs on the other. Five gaps are unoccupied in everything this research found. No surface accepts cards from an arbitrary agent. No shortlist persists outside one vendor's conversation or catalog. No shared card schema spans products, hotels and tickets. No standard carries a human's picks, rejections and notes back to an agent as structured input. No neutral tool groups a multi-merchant shortlist into the separate checkouts that Shopify's one-checkout-per-merchant rule requires. Neutrality is itself a position: Anthropic promises no paid placements, while OpenAI and Amazon are building sponsored cards into the conversational shelf, and the Honey and Phia episodes left affiliate-funded shopping tools with a trust problem. These absence claims rest on one day of web and GitHub searches. The research did not check the Claude or ChatGPT connector directories directly, Product Hunt, or recent YC batches, and a quietly shipped feature could have been missed. Whether Claude Artifacts or ChatGPT Canvas are already being used as ad hoc comparison boards was also not checked.

Four risks are serious. The first is absorption. Google already owns both halves (Canvas as a side-panel workspace and Universal Cart as a persistent multi-retailer container) and has only to join them; ChatGPT has Favorites; Anthropic's November side-panel apps list Shopify as in development; Daydream's rack shows a vertical player can ship the whole loop. The second is weak standalone pull. Onton's founder called the canvas the easy part, Carted and Edge Collections died, and no user research exists for a cross-agent board. The third is distribution. The canvas works only when an agent chooses to call its tools, hosts control widget rendering and bridge APIs that are still changing, and a custom connector gets no in-product discovery. The fourth is data. Amazon blocks outside agents, UCP states that catalog responses "are not transactional commitments" ([UCP Catalog spec](https://ucp.dev/latest/specification/shopping/catalog/)), and mismatched prices and stale availability are among the main ways AI shopping tools lose trust, so cards need visible timestamps and sources. Monetization is a fifth constraint more than a risk: affiliate income is small, structurally at odds with agent-driven journeys, and dangerous to pursue aggressively.

As an open-source portfolio project the path is clear and low-risk. The buildable core is one remote MCP server with a small tool surface (add and update cards, read the shortlist), one MCP App view that requests fullscreen for comparing and stays within the 3-to-8-card carousel inline, server-side board state behind a shareable URL for agents that cannot render UI, and a card model that adopts UCP's product and cart shapes and adds project-defined hotel and ticket types. Shopify's keyless catalog supplies real demo data, and checkout is a merchant permalink. The evidence sets the design constraints: compare at most about five items at once, require no account, show price and stock with retrieval time, group by merchant, mirror a full snapshot of the selection to the model, and treat listing text as untrusted. Such a project demonstrates protocol fluency, interaction design for the least-served part of the funnel, and a security posture, and it fills a gap where the nearest open-source neighbor is an archived 5-star demo. It should be judged as a reference implementation and a design argument, since GitHub interest in shopper-side tools is low.

As a product the path is harder and needs a wedge beyond the board. Three options fit the evidence. A hosted tier (accounts, cross-device sync, price and availability tracking on shortlisted items) follows the one open-source monetization pattern with support, with clearly disclosed, human-clicked affiliate links as incidental revenue. A vertical start in a category where people insist on choosing for themselves and the agent then executes, such as hotels or tickets, matches Accenture's finding on identity and enjoyment purchases and the weak standards coverage that leaves incumbents slower there; the research found no demand data for tickets specifically. A component for agent builders is the third option, since infrastructure startups and new personal agents own no decision surface; this is an inference from Rye's map and funding patterns, with no evidence of buyer demand. In each case the defensible asset is the hand-back loop and the cross-agent schema. The grid of cards is the part every host can replicate.

## Conclusion

The research changes the framing of the canvas idea in one important way: the scarce thing is the return path, and the visual surface is secondary. Every host can draw cards, and several now keep them. None lets a person's deliberate choice (these three, not that one, this note) travel as structured data to whichever agent holds the wallet. The industry has converged on "agent prepares, human approves, merchant stays merchant of record" for payment, and it has built that approval as a one-item confirmation sheet. A canvas extends the same human-in-the-loop principle upstream to the choice itself, which is where consumers say they want control and where 2026's failures (single-item checkout, three-times-lower in-chat conversion, wrongly executed bookings) occurred.

The timing argument cuts both ways. Agent wallets arrived only in the last ten weeks, so for the first time a selection handed back to an agent can end in a completed purchase without the canvas touching payment. The same ten weeks produced Universal Cart, Favorites, Daydream's rack and Claude's side-panel apps, so the window in which the decision layer is unclaimed is short. The most valuable next evidence is cheap to gather: a working prototype tested on a phone inside Claude and ChatGPT, and a handful of observed sessions of people choosing a hotel or a ticket with it, would answer the two questions this research could not, namely whether hosts render and round-trip the selection reliably on mobile and whether people use a board when an agent fills it.
